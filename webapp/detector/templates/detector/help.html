FAQ & troubleshooting

Help

Answers to common questions about uploading, reading results, and what Detectra can and can't tell you.

Uploading
What file formats and sizes are supported?

Video: .mp4, .avi, .mov, .mkv, up to 100 MB. Image: .jpg, .jpeg, .png, up to 10 MB. Exact limits are shown on the Settings page, since they can vary by deployment.

Why was my upload rejected?

Usually one of three things: the file exceeds the size limit, the extension isn't in the supported list, or the file is empty/corrupted. All of this is checked before the file is saved or analyzed, so a rejection is instant.

If the file passed validation but analysis still failed, it's most likely that no face could be detected in it — a clearly visible, front-facing face is required.

Is my uploaded file stored permanently?

No. The original video or image is deleted immediately after inference completes. What is kept permanently is the prediction record — the verdict, confidence scores, and the small face/Grad-CAM grid images generated during analysis — so the result stays viewable at a stable URL and downloadable as a PDF later.

Reading results
What do REAL, FAKE, and UNCERTAIN mean?

REAL or FAKE means the model's top prediction cleared the 75% confidence threshold. UNCERTAIN means neither class reached 75% — the media is ambiguous to the model, often due to low quality, partial face visibility, unusual lighting, or heavy compression. An uncertain result is not a third class; it's the model declining to force a call it isn't confident about.

Why is single-image mode less reliable than video?

Detectra's model expects a sequence of frames so its LSTM can look for frame-to-frame inconsistency over time. A single image has no time axis, so the one detected face is repeated 20 times to satisfy that input shape — the LSTM sees no real motion, and the verdict rests entirely on spatial (single-frame) features. Video mode uses genuine temporal analysis and is the more reliable mode whenever a video is available. See How it works for the full explanation.

What is the Grad-CAM heatmap showing me?

Red/yellow regions are the pixels that most influenced the model's decision; blue/dark regions had little influence. It commonly lights up around blending boundaries — jawline, hairline, eyes, teeth — which is where deepfake generation artifacts typically concentrate. It explains where the CNN looked, not how the LSTM weighed information across frames.

How accurate is the model?

90.77% accuracy on a held-out test set, with full precision/recall/F1/AUC-ROC figures and confusion matrix on the Model details page. That page also explains the dataset size limitation those numbers were measured on.

Can I download or share a result?

Every result has a permanent URL (/result/<id>/) and a "Download PDF report" button containing the verdict, confidence breakdown, face/Grad-CAM grids, and analysis metadata. The Reports page lists recent analyses with one-click PDF downloads.
